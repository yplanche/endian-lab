BIOGEOCHEMISTRY / JOURNAL ARTICLE

Optimising nonlinear calibration of glass pH electrodes over the pH range −4.5 to 15.5 using improved statistical treatment of acid and alkaline errors

Saleesongsom S, Plancherel Y, Weiss D

Talanta · Published online 20 August 2026 · Issue February 2027

Glass pH electrodes lose accuracy in strongly acidic and alkaline solutions. This study compares seven nonlinear calibration models and identifies an asymmetric double-logistic function that describes electrode behaviour from pH −4.5 to 15.5 at 25 °C. Compared with conventional three-point calibration, it reduces extreme-pH bias by up to 3.0 units in acidic solutions and 1.5 units in alkaline solutions. Statistical tests identify a smaller set of calibration standards that maintains accuracy within ±0.25 pH unit at the extremes while cutting calibration time by at least half.

01 / SPACE TO SURFACE

Earth observation
& environmental AI

We develop ways to turn satellite imagery, drone observations and environmental data into insight on changing landscapes. Directions include ecosystem and natural capital monitoring, combining multispectral imagery with 3D LiDAR point clouds, and detecting resource extraction. These examples evolve as new observations, methods and environmental questions emerge.

02 / OCEAN TO COAST

Oceans, climate
& changing coastlines

We investigate how ocean circulation, climate variability and coastal processes shape marine environments and low-lying reef islands. Illustrative directions include water-mass transport, island morphodynamics and coastal hazards in the Indian Ocean, combining field observations, remote sensing and numerical models. New questions connect processes across scales, from ocean basins to individual coastlines.

03 / ELEMENTS TO ECOSYSTEMS

Environmental
biogeochemistry

We investigate the movement and transformation of elements through environmental systems, using trace elements, isotopes, laboratory measurements and models. Examples range from ocean biogeochemistry and environmental sources and sinks to chemical measurement methods and mine-water treatment. These directions provide a starting point for new questions about chemical processes and environmental change.

04 / RESOURCES TO SYSTEMS

Resource flows
& industrial ecology

We develop models that connect material stocks, metal cycles and economic dynamics to understand resource use and the low-carbon transition. Research directions include dynamic and probabilistic representations of production, trade, use and recycling, with isotope geochemistry offering additional constraints. These approaches open further questions about resource movements, circularity and changing industrial systems.
